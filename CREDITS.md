# QuietLoop — Audio sources, craft and licensing

Existing recorded layers use edited real field or physical-instrument recordings. The version 3.3 additions combined recordings, two pitch-adjusted fork derivatives, and a custom physical-model sound design. Version 4.2.4 replaces that Monochord design with the physical-instrument recording credited below. The app packages Android-supported audio assets; full-resolution masters and original downloaded source files are retained under `audio_masters/`. Atmospheres use prepared decoder handoffs at loop boundaries. No sound was ripped from Spotify.

Kalimba adaptation: the packaged AAC retains every intentional note gap from the credited source and applies +5.5 dB level only, with no compression or trimming.

QuietLoop 4.2.3 audio repair: Soft rain, Roof rain, Distant thunder, Gentle wind, and Campfire retain their existing recordings and complete timing. They received individual fixed gain and peak limiting, with periodic context at loop boundaries, followed by AAC encoding. Shoreline and Frogs use the replacement sources credited above. These seven assets target -22 LUFS (within 2 LU) with encoded true peaks at or below -2 dBTP; their previous runtime gain compensation is disabled. Original inputs, downloaded previews, 24-bit WAV masters, processing settings and hashes are retained in `audio_masters/repairs/2026-11-07/` and `audits/2026-11-07-audio-repair/`. Brown noise's own output scaling was corrected to prevent output clipping; Blue noise now differentiates the existing pink-noise filter to produce the expected rising spectrum. Other sound generators are unchanged.

Artwork: a dedicated sound-specific tile image for each released sound. Most are custom-generated photorealistic images produced for QuietLoop, distributed as optimized WebP assets.

Changes to recordings: cropped and resampled. Original licenses still apply. Attribution, adaptations and share-alike obligations do not disappear because the app is private. The 25 noise-color, fan, motor, transport and similar textures are procedurally synthesized. The 128 Hz and 256 Hz fork entries are adapted from the genuine CC0 440 Hz fork recording above. Binaural playback tones are generated by the current app engine. Monochord uses the physical-instrument recording credited above. No sound is a guaranteed tinnitus treatment. Any remaining reserved acoustic entries stay disabled until their source and processing are audited.

QuietLoop 4.2.4 audio repair: Gong and Monochord use the replacement recordings credited above, targeting -22 LUFS with no additional runtime gain. Snow wind and its Warm mask alias retain the full existing recording with -9.1 dB fixed gain. Runtime compensation is +1.0 dB for the 128 Hz fork, +1.8 dB for the 256 Hz fork and +2.8 dB for Japanese rin, in both configured gain maps. Pink noise, Fan, HVAC and Airplane generator outputs are scaled by 0.75 before their output clamp; their filters and random seeds are preserved. Source metadata, exact recipes, source hashes and measured output levels are retained in the dated audit and repair folders. These technical checks do not establish firsthand listening acceptance.
